<objective>
Give the iOS mirror recovery job the same exact-identity authorization gate its three sibling irreversible jobs already carry, and make a scanner check hold it there.

Purpose: `recover-ios-mirror` is the only mode able to replace the public mirror's `main` with a leased force push, and it is the only irreversible job in the Phase 168 authority chain with no hardcoded identity validation. Forensics confirm it has never run non-skipped, so this is a latent hole rather than the path the live publish took — it is closed on its merits, at routine priority.
Output: An authorization step ahead of credentials and checkout, a structural check that fails closed if it disappears, and a runbook statement of the recovery mode's scope.
</objective>

<interfaces>
- The gate pattern to copy is the `Validate exact Phase 168 iOS publication authority` step at the top of the `publish-ios-mirror` job in `.github/workflows/ios-mirror-backfill.yml`: a first step with no `uses:`, carrying the approved identities as hardcoded step-level environment values and comparing each dispatch input against them under `set -euo pipefail`. The same shape appears in `.github/workflows/hex-publish.yml` on both the `publish` recovery job and the `recover-android-core` job.
- The approved Phase 168 identity constants already present in those jobs are the merge OID, approved head, approved tree, approved base, candidate receipt digest, mirror main, and mirror split. Read their exact values from the `publish-ios-mirror` job rather than retyping them from anywhere else.
- Recovery differs from publication in exactly one input: the lease value. Publication can hardcode the expected old mirror ref because it knows the pre-publication mirror state; recovery exists precisely because mirror `main` has diverged to an unknown commit, so its expected old ref must remain the freshly observed value and can only be shape-checked, not pinned. Everything else about the transaction is knowable and must be pinned. D-25 keeps leased replacement in a separately invoked, explicitly approved path; this gate is what makes "explicitly approved" mechanical.
- `script/check_release_workflow_integrity.exs` builds one list of `check/3` results; `job_blocks/1` plus `job_block/2` scope a scan to a single job body. `phase168_partial_recovery_routes/3` is the closest existing example of binding jobs to the approved identity constants. The scanner currently reports 20 checks, all OK.
- `Crosswake.ReleaseStatus` shells out to this scanner and surfaces named check ids, so a new check id must not be added to any existing required-id list unless that list is also updated; adding a standalone check is the lower-risk path.
